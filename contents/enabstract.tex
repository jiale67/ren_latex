\enabstract Complex dynamic environments, including manufacturing facilities, warehouses, construction sites, energy facilities, mines, and emergency-response areas, are often characterized by dense equipment, confined passages, irregular obstacles, mixed traffic involving personnel and mobile equipment, and limited availability of satellite positioning. Conventional manual operations have limitations in safety, response speed, and continuous operation. Mobile robots operating in such environments therefore require multisource perception, reliable localization, dynamic-obstacle understanding, and real-time path planning. To address these requirements, this thesis investigates the design and application of a mobile robot navigation system for complex dynamic environments.

First, a sensing configuration centered on a three-dimensional LiDAR and a stereo depth camera is established, and transformations among the world, robot-base, LiDAR, and camera coordinate frames are derived to spatially unify the multisensor data. Intrinsic calibration of the stereo depth camera and static extrinsic configuration of the sensors are completed. Depth back-projection, point-cloud transformation, sensor-specific voxel fusion, and temporal decay are then used to unify LiDAR and depth-camera observations into a stable local point-cloud representation.

Second, to address repetitive structures, local geometric degeneracy, and accumulated drift during long-term operation in complex indoor and confined environments, a prebuilt point-cloud map is introduced into the FAST-LIO2 LiDAR-inertial odometry framework. Continuous global localization in known environments is achieved through prebuilt-map loading, startup alignment combining an approximate initial pose with fine ICP registration, and iterative state updates constrained by the fixed map. Pure-localization and hybrid-mapping modes are provided for different operational requirements.

Third, considering limited onboard computing resources, the restricted field of view of depth cameras, and false detections produced by individual detectors in dynamic environments, a lightweight obstacle detection and tracking method combining U-depth images with DBSCAN point-cloud clustering is developed. Dynamic obstacles are identified through multi-detector association, bounding-box motion estimation, and point-cloud voting, while their velocity and acceleration are estimated using a constant-acceleration state model. Free-space updating, stale-occupancy removal, and obstacle inflation are further performed to construct a local environment representation for planning safety constraints.

Finally, a neural distance-encoded model predictive obstacle-avoidance planning method is developed for irregular, nonconvex, and dynamic obstacle environments. Obstacle avoidance and motion constraints are handled entirely within the local planner, which requires only that the upper-layer reference path be sampleable by arc length and terminate at the goal position; an A* search, an RRT, or even a straight line between the start and goal can therefore serve as the reference. The planner incorporates point-level obstacle representations, constant-acceleration predicted point flows, and a distance--separation-direction encoder, in which a lightweight neural network learns offline the mapping from an obstacle point to its distance and dual separation direction with respect to the robot convex contour. A single online forward pass then yields the distance-constraint coefficients for the entire prediction horizon, avoiding repeated solution of the distance problem while generating trajectories that satisfy motion, smoothness, and collision-avoidance constraints. For the Mecanum-wheel omnidirectional chassis used in this work, the local planner adopts a three-degree-of-freedom motion model whose control vector consists of Cartesian translational velocities in the map frame together with the angular velocity. This model is linear in both state and control, so no linearization of the dynamics is required within the receding-horizon optimization, and the heading angle is optimized jointly with the translational motion as a controllable state. Translational and angular limits are imposed as a second-order cone constraint and a box constraint respectively, the velocity cost is decomposed along the reference path direction with the lateral component discounted, and the first optimal control action is converted to body-frame velocity commands by a single planar rotation. The method is evaluated in Gazebo in a static environment and a complex dynamic environment, and is further deployed and validated on a self-developed mobile robot platform.
